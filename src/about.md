  <section class="about-section"><div class="about-section__inner">
    <h2>Introduction</h2><div class="zigzag" aria-hidden="true"></div>
    <p>Welcome to “A Third Library is Possible,” the CLIR Postdoctoral Fellowship Curated Futures Project!</p>
    <p>Focusing on curation and collaboration, “curated futures” describes the possibilities opened by imagination and speculation. The project brings together a wide variety of work reflecting the interests, experience, and creative goals of collaborators across the CLIR Postdoctoral Fellowship community.</p>
    <p>Curated Futures creates room for freedom and expression in both the contributions and the collaborative process. Scholars and digital cultural heritage practitioners from different institutions and areas of interest were invited to experiment with less conventional forms of scholarly work.</p>
    <p>Building on CLIR’s earlier collaborative projects, this collection asks readers to reconsider how we work with research, libraries, archives, and museums. Its digital form responds to a cultural environment shaped by disconnection, uncertainty, and rapidly changing needs.</p>
  </div></section>
  <section class="about-section about-section--cream"><div class="about-section__inner">
    <h2>Defining the “Third Library”</h2><div class="zigzag zigzag--orange" aria-hidden="true"></div>
    <p>The Third Library is imagined as an ethical, socially just, and hope-filled ecosystem of knowledge and cultural production. It challenges what we have come to know about libraries of the past while creating a library for the very near future.</p>
    <p>Rather than treating the future as a distant utopia, the project takes a practical approach to speculation grounded in technology, infrastructure, history, and activism. It asks what we want from future institutions and how we can begin building them now.</p>
  </div></section>
  <section class="about-section"><div class="about-section__inner">
    <h2>Afrofuturism and Speculative Futures</h2><div class="zigzag" aria-hidden="true"></div>
    <p>Afrofuturism reimagines time and space through fantasy and technology. Applied to digital humanities and cultural heritage work, it offers a framework for centering communities historically excluded from world-making.</p>
    <p>Through this lens, digital tools can support more open, collaborative, and socially engaged approaches to research data, collections, teaching, and community partnerships.</p>
  </div></section>
  <section class="about-section about-section--cream"><div class="about-section__inner">
    <h2>Introducing the Contributions</h2><div class="zigzag zigzag--orange" aria-hidden="true"></div>
    <p>The collection includes essays, interviews, podcasts, maps, visualizations, games, and immersive experiences. Each contribution offers a different practical or speculative response to the conviction that a Third Library is possible.</p>
    <p>Taken together, the works model how scholarship, libraries, museums, archives, and other cultural institutions can participate in a future built through acknowledgment, collaboration, social justice, and change.</p>
    <p><em>(January 2022)</em></p>
